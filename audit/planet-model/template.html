What runs

  • Relief: the canon rescaled elevation (z_ki, 6 arcmin) put through the Earth–Ki-Engir map transform and averaged to 2° cells.
  • Star and orbit: the orbital period sets both the day length and, through Kepler's third law and the star's luminosity, the flux. The star's colour sets how the light is split.
  • Starlight: water vapour above the cloud deck absorbs first, the cloud reflects what is left, vapour below absorbs again, and the surface takes or reflects the rest. The absorption law is the one added to ExoPlaSim for this project: the solar fit re-weighted in three near-infrared bands by the star's spectrum. Cloud and ice are dimmer under a red star, and cloud thins as the air warms.
  • Energy: that absorbed starlight against gray-atmosphere emission with CO₂ and water-vapour terms, plus heat diffusion with an extra ocean share. Solved implicitly each step.
  • Surface: air follows a 3.89 K/km lapse rate. Skin temperature comes from the surface energy balance: sunlight, back-radiation, turbulent exchange, evaporation and, under sea ice, conduction from the sea. Dry sunlit ground runs hot and night ground cold-pools on its own; slopes above a cold pool warm at the canon 25 K/km, never past the air.
  • Wind: the column's net radiative heating is carried off by ascent (the weak-temperature-gradient balance used for tidally locked daysides), so air converges under the star and subsides elsewhere. That divergent flow comes from a velocity potential; the planet's rotation, one turn per orbit, turns it, drag that grows with speed slows it, and air chilled under an inversion drains downslope as katabatic wind.
  • Water: vapour moves on that wind in flux form, so rain equals evaporation exactly. It leaves the low-level layer by an e-folding residence time, by ascent where the flow converges, and by saturation over cold or high ground. Latent heat leaves where water evaporates and returns where it rains.
  • Rivers: runoff (rain minus land evaporation, none on frozen ground) routed D8 on a 0.5° grid of the canon relief after a priority-flood fill, the vault's own method. A depression deeper than 10 m holds open water that evaporates at the local PET: it spills when inflow beats that, and otherwise ends as a terminal lake.
  • Classes: Otto's 0 °C ice and 6 °C tundra cuts, then P/PET at 0.20, 0.50 and 0.65, split at the CO₂-dependent cool line (11.7 °C at 1,000 ppm) and 27 °C.
  • Succession: bare rock, lichen and moss, grass and herbs, shrub, early woodland, climax forest. Each class caps how far a cell can go, and disturbance resets cells at random.

What is fitted

Cloud cover and albedo, cloud thinning, the greenhouse depths, heat diffusion and the vapour path are fitted to six ExoPlaSim runs at once: three orbits around Teegarden's Star and three around TRAPPIST-1, each scored on its global, dayside and nightside means, planetary albedo, open ocean and land temperature at two sun angles. Otto's gates are not part of the fit. Land recycling and vapour residence time are fitted to the GCM's rain. None of these has canon status. The rest of the surface and water physics uses Earth values or stated assumptions, with nothing fitted to the gates or the land-cover shares. The relief, the 3.89 K/km lapse rate and the 25 K/km inversion come from the vault; the stars' temperature, luminosity and mass from the NASA Exoplanet Archive.

Vegetation darkens the ground, from 0.28 for bare rock to 0.10 for forest. Stellar-kingdom plants absorb out to a 1,362 nm red edge, so they should be dark under this star.

Where it fails

A simplified climate model looks authoritative. Its numbers are reference values and cannot settle canon.

  • Diffusion cannot make the GCM's cold lobes just past the terminator, so the nightside is too smooth. This drives most of the ~17 K RMS gap. The fit is also touchy: ice albedo makes a 0.02 change in cloud albedo move the planet by 4 K.
  • Quilatria and Quedros differ by only about half a kelvin: evaporation exports a little heat from the wet coast, not the several kelvin the Surface Temperature Model's continentality term supplies. Quedros clears 18 °C because the whole band is warm, with the dayside mean free and weak heat transport.
  • Rain is scored against the GCM: the pattern correlates at about 0.7 in log space, but land gets about half the GCM's rain and the nightside gets none, against the GCM's ~280 mm/yr carried there aloft. The model has only a low-level layer, so no upper-level return flow and no storm tracks.
  • The basin under the substellar rain core still carries the planet's largest river, a few hundred thousand m³/s. The GCM puts 6,500 mm/yr on substellar land against this model's ~3,000, so the real river may be larger, not smaller.
  • The sweep is only as good as its six anchors, which are 15-year T21 runs with a shortwave scheme that tends to overstate absorption under red stars. Outside the range they span (about 1,000 to 1,300 W/m²) the curves are extrapolation, and the hot branch in particular is a guess.
  • The sweep carries one climate inward from the cold end. A planet cooling from a hot start could sit on a different branch at the same orbit, and this page does not show that one.
  • The model does not check whether an orbit is stable beside the star's real planets. The line under the star buttons says which planets it falls between.
